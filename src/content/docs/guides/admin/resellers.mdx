A **reseller** sells hosting on your servers to their own customers. You give them limits; within
those, they make their own [packages](/guides/admin/customers/#create-a-package) and customers,
and see only their own customers. Use it when a web designer, an agency or a smaller host wants
to sell hosting under their own name without running servers.

<Aside type="caution" title="New after v0.1.0">
Resellers are in the main repository's `dev` branch and come with the **next release**. A panel
on v0.1.0 does not have the **Resellers** page yet.
</Aside>

## Before you start

- You are signed in as an **administrator**. **Resellers** is under **Administration** in the
  menu, next to **Customers** and **Packages**.
- Read [Customers and packages](/guides/admin/customers/) first: a reseller's customers are
  ordinary customers, with a package each.
- Decide which servers, PHP versions and Docker images the reseller may offer.

## Add a reseller

<Steps>

1. Open **Resellers** and click **Add reseller**.

2. Enter a **Name** and an **Email**. The email address is what they sign in with.

3. Fill in the three groups of limits, described below: **Totals for all their customers**,
   **The most one package may give** and **What their packages may offer**.

4. Click **Create reseller**.

5. Copy the password: "A password is generated and shown once. You can reset it later." "Send
   the reseller this address and password. They should change the password and turn on
   two-factor after signing in."

</Steps>

The panel does not send the reseller an email; send the password yourself, over a safe channel.

### Totals for all their customers

"What all the reseller's customers may have together."

| Field | What it limits | Empty or 0 means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customers** | How many customers the reseller may have | 0 means none |
| **Websites**, **Databases**, **Apps**, **Cron jobs** | How many of each all their customers may have together (0 to 1,000,000) | 0 means none |
| **Mail domains**, **Mailboxes** | Mail domains and mailboxes over all their customers | 0 means none |
| **Disk space (GB)** | Disk space used by all their customers together | Empty: **No limit** |
| **Bandwidth per month (GB)** | What all their customers' websites and apps send in a calendar month (UTC), at least 1 GB. See [Bandwidth](#bandwidth) | Empty: **No limit** |
| **Stop all their customers' websites and apps when it is used up** | Whether every customer's websites and app addresses stop once the bandwidth is used up | Off. Can only be ticked with a bandwidth limit |

### The most one package may give

"Empty means any. With a limit, every package of the reseller must have one at most this large."

| Field | What it limits |
| --- | --- |
| **Memory (MB)** | The memory limit of each of the reseller's packages, at least 64 MB |
| **CPU (cores)** | The CPU limit of each package, at least 5% of a core |
| **Mailbox size (GB)** | The largest mailbox size each package may allow |

These are per package, not totals: a reseller with **Memory (MB)** `1024` may have ten customers
with 1024 MB each.

### What their packages may offer

| Field | What it limits | Empty means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PHP versions** | The PHP versions their packages may include | "None ticked: every PHP version." |
| **Servers** | The servers their packages may use. "Their customers' websites, databases and apps go only on these." | "None ticked: every server." |
| **Allowed Docker images** | The images their packages may allow. "One per line; a name ending in / allows everything under it." Shown only when **Apps** is more than 0 | "Empty: any image." |
| **SFTP access to their websites**, **FTP (encrypted) for their websites**, **Git apps** | Whether their packages may include these | Off |

### Change a reseller's limits

Click **Edit reseller** on the reseller's card, change the limits and click **Save changes**. Lower
limits stop new things. What the customers already have stays, and so do packages that are now
above the new limits; the reseller cannot save such a package again without lowering it. A new
bandwidth limit is checked at once (see [Bandwidth](#bandwidth)).

If a number is not accepted: "Check the limits: counts are 0 or more, and sizes must be large
enough."

## How the totals are counted

"Their packages may promise more; what customers actually have is counted, and new things are
refused once a total is reached."

A reseller may sell more than they have, as hosts do. A reseller with a total of 10 websites
may sell twenty packages of 3 websites each. The panel counts the websites, databases, apps,
cron jobs, email domains, mailboxes and disk space that all their customers **actually have**,
and refuses a new one once a total is reached, with a message such as "The reseller's account
allows no more websites." Each customer's own package still applies too, so whichever limit is
reached first stops them.

The same check runs for everything that adds to a reseller's customers, whoever does it:

- the reseller, or the customer themselves;
- an administrator who creates something for one of their customers (**For** on the website,
  database or app form), or who [signs in as](/guides/admin/customers/#sign-in-as-a-customer)
  one of them;
- [imports](/guides/admin/import-cpanel/) for one of their customers;
- [owner changes](/guides/admin/customers/#change-the-owner) that give one of their customers
  something from outside the reseller.

**Disk space** is measured every 30 minutes, as for
[customers](/guides/admin/customers/#how-disk-space-is-measured). When the reseller's total is
used up: "The disk space of the reseller's account is used up." New websites, databases, apps,
cron jobs and mail domains are refused for all their customers, and so are file manager actions
that add data. Nothing is deleted, and websites keep running.

**Servers** limit where things go: "The reseller's account cannot use that server."

In the project's test VM, a reseller had a total of 2 websites. One customer made a website,
another customer made one, and a third was refused.

## What a reseller sees and does

A reseller signs in at the same address as you. Their **Dashboard** says "Your customers'
websites, databases and apps, and what your reseller account allows." and shows **Your reseller
account**: "What all your customers have together, against what the administrator gave you."

They see:

- **Websites**, **WordPress**, **Databases**, **Apps**, **Email** and **DNS**: everything their
  customers have, and nothing else. Anything else answers "not found", as it does for customers.
- Under **Reseller** in the menu: **Customers** and **Packages**, their own only.
- **Security**, **Notifications** and **API tokens** for their own account.

They can:

- make [their own packages](#a-resellers-packages), within the limits you gave them;
- add, change and delete their customers, give them a **New password**, **Reset two-factor**,
  **Suspend** and **Unsuspend** them, and **Sign in as** them;
- create websites, databases, apps, email domains, DNS zones and cron jobs **for** one of their
  customers;
- [change the owner](/guides/admin/customers/#change-the-owner) of things between their own
  customers.

A reseller owns nothing themselves: everything they create belongs to one of their customers.
On a form, they choose the customer under **For**. Without any customer yet, the form says "Add
a customer first: as a reseller, everything you create belongs to one of your customers." With
the API, leaving out the customer gives "Choose the customer this is for."

### A reseller's own website

To host their own website, the reseller makes a customer for themselves, with one of their
packages, and creates the website for that customer. It counts against their totals like any
other.

### What a reseller cannot do

- **Servers**, **Backups** and **Server security**: they see only the names of the servers they
  may use, to choose from. They cannot move things to another server.
- **Import**, **Updates** and the **Audit log**.
- **Resellers**: they cannot make resellers of their own.
- Connect a customer's **Cloudflare** account: "Sign in as the customer to connect their
  Cloudflare account." Use **Sign in as** on the customer, then connect it as them.
- Give things to the administrators with **Change owner…**: only to one of their own customers.
- Lift a suspension an administrator made (see [Suspend a reseller](#suspend-a-reseller)).

## A reseller's packages

Packages belong either to the administrators or to one reseller. A reseller makes theirs on
**Packages** the same way you do (see [Create a package](/guides/admin/customers/#create-a-package)),
and their customers can only have one of the reseller's packages. Package names must be unique
for each reseller, so a reseller can have a "Starter" package of their own next to yours.

A reseller's package can give **no more than the reseller has**:

- no count above the reseller's total (a package of 20 websites for a reseller with 10 is refused);
- no size above the most one package may give, and no disk space or bandwidth above the
  reseller's total;
- where the reseller's size is limited, the package's cannot be left empty, because empty means
  "no limit";
- only the PHP versions, servers and images the reseller has, and SFTP, FTP or Git apps only if
  the reseller has them.

Two exceptions: the images matter only for a package with apps, and the mailbox size only for a
package with mailboxes. A reseller whose images are limited can make a package with 0 apps
without choosing any.

If a package does not fit, the panel names the field: "This package gives more than your
reseller account allows: …. Your limits are on the dashboard; an empty size means "no limit",
which is more than a limited account allows."

Administrators see every package. The packages table then has a **Whose** column: **Yours**, or the
reseller's name. If you edit a reseller's package, it is checked against that reseller's limits
too.

## Suspend a reseller

Suspending a reseller stops them **and all their customers** at once, without deleting anything.

<Steps>

1. On **Resellers**, click **Suspend** on the reseller's card.

2. Read what it does: "The reseller and all their customers can no longer sign in, and every
   customer's websites and apps show a "suspended" page, as if each was suspended."

3. Optionally enter a **Reason**, up to 500 characters, for the administrators and the audit log.

4. Click **Suspend**.

</Steps>

The card then shows "Suspended since …". While the reseller is suspended:

- **The reseller cannot sign in**, and neither can any of their customers, with a password, a
  passkey or an API token. Their open sessions end.
- **Every customer is treated as suspended**: their websites and app addresses answer with the
  "Website suspended" page (HTTP 503), their apps stop, and so on. See
  [Suspend a customer](/guides/admin/customers/#suspend-a-customer) for everything that means.
- **Nothing is deleted.**

Click **Unsuspend** to put everything back. Customers who were suspended **on their own** (by
you or by the reseller) stay suspended.

A reseller can suspend and unsuspend their own customers, but cannot lift a suspension an
administrator made: "An administrator suspended this customer, so only an administrator can lift
it."

The audit log records "Reseller suspended" (with the reason) and "Reseller unsuspended" under
your name.

In the project's test VM, suspending a reseller turned both their customers' websites into the
"suspended" page (503) and refused the sign-in of the reseller and of both customers. A customer
an administrator had suspended stayed suspended after the reseller was unsuspended, and the
reseller could not lift it.

## Bandwidth

With **Bandwidth per month (GB)**, a reseller's bandwidth is the traffic of **all their
customers' websites and apps added up**, per calendar month in UTC. It is counted and checked
like a [customer's bandwidth](/guides/admin/customers/#how-bandwidth-is-counted): every 15
minutes, when traffic is collected, and at once when you change the reseller's limits or move a
customer between resellers.

The reseller and the administrators get a [notification](/guides/admin/notifications/) at 90%
("Bandwidth almost used up") and at 100% ("Bandwidth used up"), once a month each.

At 100%:

- **Without** "Stop all their customers' websites and apps when it is used up": nothing stops. It
  is only a warning.
- **With** it: the notification is "Bandwidth used up, websites stopped", and every customer's
  websites and app addresses answer with the "Bandwidth used up" page (HTTP 503) until the next
  month. The reseller's card shows "Bandwidth used up: websites stopped", and each of their
  customers shows "Reseller's bandwidth used up: websites stopped" on the customers list.

The websites start again at the first check of the next month, or at once when you raise the
reseller's limit above what was used or untick the stop. Each customer's own package limit
applies as well: a customer can be stopped by their own package while the reseller still has
bandwidth left.

In the project's test VM, two customers of a reseller with 1 GB served 600 MB and 457 MB, each
under their own 1 GB package. Together, 1.06 GB stopped both websites with the bandwidth page
and told the reseller. Raising the reseller to 5 GB brought both back within 2 seconds.

## Notifications for resellers

A reseller gets the alerts about their customers' things, as well as the customer: a website,
app, database or email problem, a failed deploy or cron job, malware found, a customer's disk
space or bandwidth, and so on. They also get their own bandwidth alerts (above), and the alerts
about their own account (a sign-in from a new address, a recovery code used). They set up their
own channels under **Notifications**, like everyone else. See
[Notifications](/guides/admin/notifications/).

## For administrators: resellers' customers

The customers list shows every customer, with "via …" under the name of each reseller's customer.

- **Show only some customers.** Choose under **Show**: **All customers**, **Your own customers**,
  or **Customers of …** for one reseller. The **Customers** button on a reseller's card opens the
  list for that reseller.
- **Add a customer for a reseller.** In **Add customer**, choose the **Reseller**. "A reseller's
  customer uses one of that reseller's packages and counts against the reseller's limits." The
  **Package** list then shows that reseller's packages.
- **Move a customer to another reseller, or to yourself.** Click **Edit** on the customer,
  choose another **Reseller**, or **None (your own customer)**, and a package of theirs. Before
  anything changes, the panel checks that the new reseller has room for one more customer and for
  everything the customer has (websites, databases, apps, cron jobs, email, disk space and
  servers). Nothing moves on the server. Bandwidth is checked again for both resellers.
- **Sign in as a reseller.** Click **Sign in as** on the reseller's card to see the panel as they
  do, as for a [customer](/guides/admin/customers/#sign-in-as-a-customer). The audit log records
  "Signed in as reseller". While you are signed in as a reseller you cannot also sign in as one
  of their customers ("Go back to your own account first."): go back, then use **Sign in as** on
  the customer.
- **New password** and **Reset two-factor** work for a reseller as for a
  [customer](/guides/admin/customers/#reset-a-password-or-two-factor-sign-in).

In the project's test VM, an owner change moved a website between a reseller's customers, and
the website kept serving.

### Delete a reseller

A reseller who still has customers cannot be deleted: "This reseller still has customers. Move
them to yourself or another reseller, or delete them, first." Move them with **Edit** on each
customer, or delete them. Then click the delete button on the reseller's card. "The reseller's
account and packages go."

In the project's test VM, deleting a reseller was refused while they had customers; once the
customers were gone, it removed the reseller and their package.

## API tokens and the API

Resellers have their own [API tokens](/guides/security/api-tokens/) under **API tokens**, with
eight scopes: **Read**, **Websites**, **Databases**, **Apps**, **DNS**, **Email**, **Customers**
and **Settings**. A reseller's token sees only their customers and their customers' things, like
the reseller in the browser. With **Customers** it can manage their customers and packages and
change owners between their customers (`/customers`, `/packages` and `/owner-changes`).

Administrators manage resellers with `/api/v1/resellers` (list, add), `/api/v1/resellers/{id}`
(read, change, delete), and `POST` to `/password`, `/2fa/reset`, `/impersonate`, `/suspend` (with
`{"reason": "…"}`) and `/unsuspend` below it. A token needs the `customers` scope; resetting
two-factor sign-in and signing in as someone need a browser session. See the
[API reference](/reference/api/#administrators-and-resellers).

## Limits and what is not there yet

- **White-label** (the panel's name, logo, colours and domain for a reseller) and **sub-users**
  are not there yet. They are the next parts of phase 4e on the [roadmap](/project/roadmap/).
- Resellers cannot make resellers of their own, and there is no email to new resellers or
  customers with their sign-in details.
- The "Website suspended" and "Bandwidth used up" pages are in English and do not carry the
  reseller's name.
- Disk space is measured every 30 minutes and traffic collected every 15 minutes, so a reseller's
  customers can go somewhat over a total before new things are refused or websites stop.
- Totals count what customers have, so a reseller can sell more than their totals. If they do,
  their customers may be refused before their packages are used up; that is the reseller's
  business to manage.
