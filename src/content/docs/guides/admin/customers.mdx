If you host websites for other people, give each of them a **customer** account. A customer signs
in to the same panel and sees only what they own, within the limits of their **package**. A
package is a plan, such as "Starter" or "Business": how many websites, databases and apps, how much
disk space, and how much memory and CPU for all their processes together.

## Before you start

- You are signed in as an **administrator**. Customers and packages are under **Administration**
  in the menu.
- At least one server is connected. See [Add a server](/start/add-server/).

## Create a package

<Steps>

1. Open **Packages** and click **Add package**.

2. Enter a **Name**, such as `Starter`.

3. Fill in the limits (see the table below). For example: 3 websites, 3 databases, 1 app,
   10 cron jobs, 10 GB disk, 1024 MB memory, 1 CPU core.

4. Click **Create package**.

</Steps>

### Package limits

| Field | What it limits | Empty or 0 means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Websites**, **Databases**, **Apps**, **Cron jobs** | How many of each the customer may have (0 to 100,000). Websites made for WordPress staging count too | 0 means none. "0 for apps means no Docker apps." |
| **Mail domains**, **Mailboxes** | Mail domains and mailboxes over all the customer's domains | 0 means none |
| **Mailbox size (GB)** | The largest size of one mailbox | Empty: no limit |
| **Disk space (GB)** | Website files, app folders and volumes, databases and mail together | Empty: unlimited |
| **Memory (MB)** | Memory for **all** the customer's PHP and app processes together, at least 64 MB | Empty: no limit |
| **CPU (cores)** | CPU for all of them together, for example `0.5` or `2`, at least 5% of a core | Empty: no limit |
| <Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> **Bandwidth per month (GB)** | What the customer's websites and apps send in a calendar month (UTC), at least 1 GB. See [How bandwidth is counted](#how-bandwidth-is-counted) | Empty: no limit |
| <Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> **Stop websites and apps when it is used up, until the next month** | Whether websites and app addresses stop answering once the bandwidth is used up. Without it, the customer and the administrators are only told | Off. Always off without a bandwidth limit |
| **PHP versions** | Which PHP versions their websites may use | "None ticked means every version." |
| **Servers** | Where the customer may create things | "None ticked means every server." |
| **Allowed Docker images** | Which images their apps and catalogue services may use. One per line: a name such as `nginx` (any tag), or a prefix ending in `/` such as `ghcr.io/acme/` | "Empty means any image." |
| **SFTP access to their websites** | Whether they can turn on SFTP | Off |
| **FTP (encrypted) for their websites** | Whether they can turn on FTP | Off |
| **Git apps** | Whether they can build and deploy their own repositories ([Git deploys](/guides/apps/git-deploys/)) | Off |

Customers can never run their own Compose files: "Customers cannot use Compose files." They can
run single images and the reviewed services of the [catalogue](/guides/apps/catalog/).

### Changing a package

Edit a package and save it. New memory and CPU limits reach the customers' servers at once,
**without restarting** anything. If you lower a count, what customers already have stays; they
just cannot add more. A new bandwidth limit is checked at once too: websites stopped for
bandwidth start again if the new limit is above what was used this month, and are stopped if
it is below.

A package that still has customers cannot be deleted: "move its customers to another package
first".

## Add a customer

<Steps>

1. Open **Customers** and click **Add customer**.

2. Enter their name and email address, and choose a **Package**.

3. Click **Create customer**.

4. Copy the password: "The customer's password, shown only this once. Send it to them over a safe
   channel." They can change it under **Security** after signing in.

</Steps>

The customer's panel language starts as yours at the moment you create them; they can change it.

The customers list shows each customer's package and use, such as "2 of 3" websites and their disk
space. From the next release it also shows **Bandwidth this month**, and badges for customers who
are [suspended](#suspend-a-customer) or whose websites are stopped because their bandwidth is used
up ("Bandwidth used up: websites stopped").

### Reset a password or two-factor sign-in

If a customer loses their password, click **New password** on the customers list to make a new one
(shown once). This signs them out everywhere. It does not change their two-factor sign-in.

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> If a customer turned on two-factor sign-in and lost
both their phone and their [recovery codes](/guides/security/accounts/#recovery-codes), you can
turn it off for them:

<Steps>

1. On the customers list, click **Reset two-factor** next to the customer. The button shows only
   for customers who have two-factor sign-in on.

2. Confirm: "Turn off two-factor sign-in for …? They sign in with their password and can turn it
   on again. Do this only when you know it is really them."

3. Tell the customer to sign in with their password and turn two-factor sign-in on again under
   **Security**. They get ten new recovery codes then.

</Steps>

Resetting also deletes their recovery codes. It does not remove their passkeys and does not sign
them out. The audit log records it as "Customer's two-factor reset" under your name. If you think someone
else has the customer's password, give them a **New password** as well, which signs out every
device.

Only customers have this button. An administrator who lost their phone uses a recovery code, a
passkey, or `ekpanel reset-2fa` on the panel's server: see
[If you lose your phone](/guides/security/accounts/#if-you-lose-your-phone).

### Creating things for a customer

When you add a website, database or app, choose the customer under **For**. It then belongs to
them and counts toward their package, exactly as if they had made it.

## What customers see and do

A customer signs in at the same address as you. They see:

- **Dashboard** with "Your package: …", their use of each limit, and "Traffic this month: …".
  From the next release that line is **Bandwidth this month**, with a meter when the package has
  a bandwidth limit (otherwise "… used, no limit") and a note on how it is counted.
- **Websites**, **Databases**, **Apps**, **Email** and **DNS**: only what they own. Mail domains
  must be domains of their own websites; hosted DNS zones must be domains of their own websites or
  apps. They can connect their own Cloudflare account.
- **Security** (their password and two-factor authentication; from the next release also
  recovery codes, passkeys and their signed-in devices: see
  [Account security](/guides/security/accounts/)) and **Notifications** (their own alert
  channels).
- **API tokens** (from the next release): their own [tokens](/guides/security/api-tokens/) for
  scripts and ekctl, limited to read, websites, databases, apps, DNS and email.

They do **not** see: other customers' things, **Backups**, **Servers** (only a list of server names
to choose from, limited by their package), **Server security**, **Customers**, **Packages** or the
**Audit log**. They cannot open a server terminal.

Anything a customer may not see answers "not found", so nobody can probe for other people's
websites by trying IDs. Ownership is checked in one place for every route; an automated test walks
22 routes as another customer and expects "not found" on each.

## How memory and CPU limits are enforced

Limits are real, not just numbers in the panel. On each server, every customer gets a **systemd
slice** (a Linux control group, cgroup v2) called `ekpanel-c<customer id>.slice` with the package's
`MemoryMax` and `CPUQuota`, and at most 4,096 processes. Everything the customer runs goes inside
it:

- **PHP**: their websites run under a PHP-FPM master of their own, one per PHP version, inside
  the slice.
- **Docker apps and catalogue services**: every container gets the slice as its cgroup parent.
- **Git builds**: builds run in the slice too.
- **Cron jobs** of their websites and apps.

CPU is given in percent of one core: `1` core in the panel is `CPUQuota=100%`, `0.5` is 50%.

When a customer's processes together need more memory than the package allows, the kernel stops
one of **their** processes, not someone else's. A PHP script over the limit costs only its own
worker; the customer's other requests keep working. In the project's test VM, with a 256 MB
package, a script asking for 400 MB was stopped alone, and a 50% CPU limit gave two busy loops
2 seconds of CPU in 4 seconds.

Administrators' own websites and apps are not in any slice and have no package.

## How disk space is measured

Disk space is **measured by the panel**, not by filesystem quotas:

- Every **30 minutes**, each server's agent adds up the website home folders, and the app folders
  and Docker volumes. Each file counts once, links are not followed.
- **Database** sizes are reported each time the server applies its databases.
- **Mailbox** use comes from the mail server.

When the disk space is used up:

- New websites, databases, apps, cron jobs and mail domains are refused: "the disk space of the
  package is used up".
- File manager actions that add data (upload, new file or folder, unzip, zip) are refused.
- Nothing is deleted, and websites keep running.

The customer and the administrators get a [notification](/guides/admin/notifications/) at 90%
("Disk space almost used up") and at 100% ("Disk space used up").

<Aside type="caution">
Because space is measured every 30 minutes, a customer can go somewhat over the limit between two
measurements. **SFTP and FTP uploads are not stopped** by the disk limit, and a running
website or app can keep writing. Only the panel's own actions are refused.
</Aside>

## How bandwidth is counted

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" />

A package can limit how much data a customer's websites and apps send each month, with
**Bandwidth per month (GB)**.

- **What counts:** what the web server sends for the customer's websites and app addresses, as
  in the **Bandwidth** of the [traffic charts](/guides/websites/logs-traffic/#traffic). Email,
  SFTP and FTP, and what apps download, do not count.
- **The month** is a calendar month in UTC. It starts again at 00:00 UTC on the 1st (06:00 in
  Bangladesh).
- **When it is checked:** the panel collects traffic from every server every 15 minutes and
  compares each customer's month with their package right after. It also checks at once when you
  change a package, a customer's package, or move websites or apps between customers with
  [Change owner](#change-the-owner) (both the old and the new owner are checked).
- **Who owns the traffic:** this month's traffic goes with a website or app. If you give a
  website to another customer, what it sent this month counts for the new owner.

The customer and the administrators get a [notification](/guides/admin/notifications/) at 90%
("Bandwidth almost used up") and at 100% ("Bandwidth used up"). Each is sent once a month.

What happens at 100% depends on the package:

- **Without** "Stop websites and apps when it is used up, until the next month": nothing stops.
  The customer can go over the limit; it is only a warning.
- **With** it: the notification is "Bandwidth used up: websites stopped", and the customer's
  websites and app addresses answer every request with a short page, "Bandwidth used up", and
  HTTP status 503. Apps keep running, and SFTP, FTP, cron jobs, databases and email keep
  working. The customers list marks them "Bandwidth used up: websites stopped".

The websites start again by themselves at the first check of the next month (within 15 minutes
of 00:00 UTC on the 1st), or at once when you give the customer a larger package, raise the
package's limit above what they used, or untick the stop setting.

<Aside type="caution">
Because traffic is collected every 15 minutes, a customer can go over the limit by what their
websites send in up to 15 minutes before they are stopped.
</Aside>

In the project's test VM, a website that had served 1.1 GB with a 1 GB package switched to the
"Bandwidth used up" page; raising the package to 2 GB brought it back within 15 seconds.

## Sign in as a customer

To see exactly what a customer sees, or to fix something for them:

<Steps>

1. On the customers list, click **Sign in as** next to the customer.

2. A banner shows "You are signed in as … Everything you do is recorded under your own name."

3. Work as the customer. When you are done, click **Back to my account**.

</Steps>

- This lasts **one hour** at most.
- The audit log records each action with **your** name and that you acted as the customer.
- The customer's own account security stays theirs: while signed in as them you cannot change their
  password, turn two-factor authentication on or off, see their recovery codes, passkeys or
  signed-in devices, sign out their other sessions, change their notification channels, or see
  or make their API tokens.
- From the next release, the customer sees your visit in their **Signed-in devices** list,
  marked **An administrator**, and can sign it out there.
- Terminals, including app shells, are not available while signed in as a customer.

## Suspend a customer

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" />

Suspending stops a customer's account without deleting anything, for example when a bill is
not paid or a website is being abused. Unsuspending puts everything back as it was.

<Steps>

1. On the customers list, click **Suspend** next to the customer.

2. Optionally enter a **Reason**, up to 500 characters: "For the administrators and the audit
   log; the customer does not see it."

3. Click **Suspend**.

</Steps>

The customer's card then shows "Suspended since …" and, if you gave one, "Reason: …". To end it,
click **Unsuspend**.

While a customer is suspended:

- **They cannot sign in**, with their password, a passkey or an API token. They see "This account
  is suspended. Please contact your hosting provider." (`account_suspended`). Their open
  sessions end at once.
- **Their websites and app addresses** answer every request with a short page in English,
  "Website suspended": "This website is suspended. If it is yours, please contact your hosting
  provider." The status is 503 with `Retry-After: 3600`, which tells search engines the outage
  is temporary.
- **Their apps are stopped.**
- **Their cron jobs are paused** on the server. In the panel each job keeps its own **On** or
  **Paused** setting, which applies again when you unsuspend.
- **SFTP and FTP are off** for their websites.
- **Their mailboxes cannot sign in** (mail apps and webmail): the mail server gets a random
  password that nobody knows. **Mail still arrives** and waits in the mailboxes.
- **Nothing is deleted:** files, databases and mail stay.

You can still use [Sign in as](#sign-in-as-a-customer) to look at or fix a suspended customer's
things.

The audit log records "Customer suspended" (with the reason) and "Customer unsuspended" under your
name. With the API: `POST /api/v1/customers/{id}/suspend` with `{"reason": "…"}`, and
`POST /api/v1/customers/{id}/unsuspend` (a token needs the `customers` scope).

In the project's test VM, suspending a customer gave their website the 503 page, stopped their
app's container, turned off their cron job's timer and refused their mailbox's IMAP sign-in,
while a message sent to the mailbox still arrived. Unsuspending brought all of it back.

## Change the owner

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" />

You can give websites, databases, apps, email domains and DNS zones to another customer, or to
the administrators: when a client hands a website to someone else, when you take over a
customer's things, or before you [delete a customer](#delete-a-customer). Nothing moves on the
server.

<Steps>

1. On the customers list, click **Change owner…**: in the page header, or on a customer's card
   to start with that customer.

2. Choose **From**: a customer, or **Administrators (no package)**.

3. Choose **To**: another customer (shown with their package), or **Administrators (no
   package)**.

4. Tick what to give, under **Websites**, **Databases**, **Apps**, **Email domains** and
   **DNS zones**. DNS zones are the zones on ekPanel's own [name servers](/guides/dns/name-servers/).

5. Click **Give … items**. The panel answers, for example, "3 items now belong to …".

</Steps>

Before anything changes, the panel checks:

- **The new owner's package**: how many websites, databases, apps, cron jobs (those of the
  websites and apps), email domains and mailboxes (those of the email domains) they may have,
  their disk space, and the servers they may use. If anything does not fit, nothing changes.
  The message names what does not fit, such as "The new owner's package does not allow that many
  websites." Giving things to the administrators is never limited.
- **Moves:** a website, database or app that is being copied or switched to
  [another server](/guides/websites/move/) cannot change owner: "… is being moved to another
  server. Wait until that has finished." Once the move has switched, it can, even while its old
  copy waits to be removed.

What the new owner already has is left out; if that is everything, the panel says "Choose
something the new owner does not have yet."

What changes:

- **Only the owner.** Files, data, Linux accounts, addresses, passwords and logins stay as they
  are. The website's PHP, the app's containers and their cron jobs now run in the new owner's
  [slice](#how-memory-and-cpu-limits-are-enforced), with the new owner's memory and CPU limits (or
  none, for the administrators).
- **Cron jobs, backups and this month's traffic go with a website or app.** An email domain
  takes its mailboxes and forwarders along. A database does not follow its website: tick it
  too.
- **DNS records follow the owner of the zone.** A customer's zone holds the records ekPanel adds
  for that customer's own websites, apps and email only; an administrators' zone holds
  anyone's. So give a website and its zone together. The name servers are updated afterwards.

The audit log records "Owner changed" with the names of everything given. With the API:
`POST /api/v1/owner-changes` with `{"to": "<customer ID, or empty for the administrators>",
"sites": [...], "databases": [...], "apps": [...], "mail_domains": [...], "zones": [...]}`, IDs
only, up to 1,000 at a time (a token needs the `customers` scope).

In a test VM, a website (with its cron job), an app and an email domain were given to another
customer, and the package's app limit refused a third app.

## Only administrators can

- Create, change and delete packages and customers, reset customers' passwords (and, from the
  next release, their two-factor sign-in), and sign in as them.
- From the next release: suspend and unsuspend customers, and change the owner of websites,
  databases, apps, email domains and DNS zones.
- Add and remove servers, see their metrics and services, and open a root
  [terminal](/guides/apps/terminal/).
- Set up [backups](/guides/data/backups/), the firewall, server security, email servers and the
  built-in name servers.
- Run their own Compose files and use public ports for apps.
- Set the panel's SMTP server and address for [notifications](/guides/admin/notifications/).
- Read the audit log.

Every administrator has all of these rights; there are no lesser administrator roles yet.

## Move a customer to another server

To move everything a customer has on one server (websites, databases and apps) to another server,
click **Move…** next to the customer on the customers list, then choose **Move from** and
**Move to**. The panel checks everything first and moves one thing after the other; you remove
each old copy from its own page afterwards. See [Move a customer](/guides/admin/move-customer/).

## Delete a customer

A customer who still owns something cannot be deleted: "They still have … websites, … databases and
… apps. Delete or move them first." Delete those first, or (from the next release) give them to
another customer or to the administrators with [Change owner](#change-the-owner). Then click the
delete button on the customers list.

To stop a customer for a while without losing anything, [suspend](#suspend-a-customer) them
instead.

## Limits and what is not there yet

- **Email to new customers** with their sign-in details: not yet; send the password yourself.
- **Resellers** (who sell your packages to their own customers) and **sub-users**: not yet.
- Real filesystem quotas: not yet; disk space is measured as described above.
- The "Website suspended" and "Bandwidth used up" pages are in English only, because the server
  does not know the visitor's language. They cannot be changed yet.
- Bandwidth counts only what the web server sends; a customer can go over by up to 15 minutes of
  traffic before their websites stop.
- Change owner covers DNS zones on ekPanel's own name servers, not Cloudflare zones (customers
  connect their own Cloudflare account).
