Your ekPanel account controls servers with root access, so it is worth protecting well. This
page covers two-factor sign-in, recovery codes, passkeys, alerts about new sign-ins, passwords
and signed-in devices. Everything here is under **Your account → Security** in the menu, for
administrators, customers and (from the next release) resellers alike.

<Aside type="caution" title="New after v0.1.0">
Recovery codes, resetting someone's two-factor sign-in, passkeys and the list of signed-in
devices are in the main repository's `dev` branch and come with the **next release**. They are
marked <Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> below. A panel on v0.1.0 has two-factor
sign-in without recovery codes, and only **Sign out all other devices**.
</Aside>

## Two-factor sign-in

With two-factor authentication (2FA), signing in needs a 6-digit code from an app on your phone
as well as your password. Someone who steals or guesses your password still cannot get in.

ekPanel uses standard time-based codes (TOTP), so any authenticator app works, such as Google
Authenticator, Aegis or 1Password.

### Turn it on

<Steps>

1. Open **Your account → Security**. The **Two-factor authentication** card shows
   <Badge text="Off" variant="caution" />.

2. Choose **Turn on two-factor authentication**.

3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app. If you cannot scan, type the key shown under
   "Can't scan? Type this key into the app instead:".

4. Enter the **6-digit code** the app shows and choose **Confirm and turn on**.

5. <Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> The panel shows ten **recovery codes**. Save
   them with **Copy** or **Download** (a file called `ekpanel-recovery-codes.txt`), then choose
   **I have saved them**. See [Recovery codes](#recovery-codes).

</Steps>

The card now shows <Badge text="On" variant="success" />. Nothing changes until you confirm with a
correct code, so a half-finished setup never locks you out.

From now on, after your email and password, the sign-in page asks: "Open your authenticator app
and enter the 6-digit code for ekPanel." Codes change every 30 seconds; the code just before or
after the current one is accepted too, to allow for a clock that is slightly off.

### Recovery codes

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> A recovery code lets you sign in once without your
authenticator app, for example after you lose your phone. When you turn on two-factor sign-in,
the panel makes **ten** of them, such as `k7mqx-3vh9p`, and shows them **only that once**:
"Save these recovery codes somewhere safe, away from your phone. Each one signs you in once if
you lose your authenticator app. They are shown only now."

Keep them in a password manager or print them, but not on the phone that has your
authenticator app. The panel keeps only a SHA-256 hash of each code, so nobody can read them
back later, not even an administrator.

**To sign in with a recovery code**, enter your email and password as usual. On the next step
the field is called "Code from the app, or a recovery code", with the hint "Lost your phone?
Enter one of your recovery codes." Type one of your codes there and choose **Verify**. Upper or
lower case, spaces and dashes do not matter. Each code works **once**; a code that was used
already is refused like a wrong code.

When a recovery code is used:

- You get a **Sign-in with a recovery code** alert, with the address it came from and how many
  codes are left. It appears under the bell and in the channels you chose under
  [Notifications](/guides/admin/notifications/). If it was not you, change your password, make
  new recovery codes and sign out the other devices.
- The audit log records it as "Signed in with a recovery code", with the number of codes left.

The **Two-factor authentication** card shows how many codes you have left, such as "9 codes
left. Each signs you in once instead of the app's code." With **3 or fewer** left, the line is
shown in the warning colour.

**To make new codes**, choose **Make new recovery codes**, enter your password ("Enter your
password to make new codes (the old ones stop working)") and choose **Make new recovery codes**
again. You get ten new codes, shown once as before, and **all the old ones stop working**, used
or not. Do this when few are left, or when you think someone has seen them.

<Aside type="note">
Recovery codes are for the sign-in page only. Opening a [web terminal](/guides/apps/terminal/)
asks for the code from your authenticator app; a recovery code is not accepted there.
</Aside>

### Turn it off

Choose **Turn off two-factor authentication**, enter your password, and confirm. Repeated wrong
passwords are limited, as at sign-in. Turning it off also deletes your recovery codes; if you
turn it on again later, you get ten new ones.

### If you lose your phone

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> Without your authenticator app, you have three ways
back in:

- **A recovery code**, on the sign-in page, as described [above](#recovery-codes). Once you are
  in, turn two-factor sign-in off and on again with your new phone. That also gives you ten new
  recovery codes.
- **A passkey**, if you added one: choose **Sign in with a passkey**. It needs no code. See
  [Passkeys](#passkeys).
- **Someone resets it for you.** Two-factor sign-in is then off, and you sign in with your
  password alone. Turn it on again right away under **Your account → Security**.

Who can reset it:

- **A customer's** two-factor sign-in: any administrator, with **Reset two-factor** on the
  customers list. See
  [Customers](/guides/admin/customers/#reset-a-password-or-two-factor-sign-in).
- **An administrator's** own, or anyone's: someone with root access to the panel's server, with
  a command. There is no button for this in the panel, so a person who has only an
  administrator's password cannot use it. On the server where the panel runs, type:

  ```bash
  sudo -u ekpanel ekpanel reset-2fa admin@example.com
  ```

  Use the email address of the account. The command reads the panel's settings from
  `/etc/ekpanel/panel.env` by itself, and prints:

  ```text
  Two-factor sign-in is off for admin@example.com. Sign in with the password and turn it on again.
  ```

  If it answers "no account with the email …", check the address. The audit log records the
  reset as "Two-factor reset on the server", with `"via": "cli"`.

Resetting removes the account's recovery codes too. It does not remove its passkeys, and it does
not sign out its devices. API tokens can never reset two-factor sign-in.

<Aside type="danger" title="Keep a way back in">
- Save your recovery codes when the panel shows them, away from your phone.
- Give a second administrator their own account with two-factor sign-in, so the panel is never
  in one phone's hands.
- Keep your SSH access to the panel's server: for an administrator, it is the last way back in.
</Aside>

### Every administrator should use it

The checklist on the [Server security](/guides/security/overview/) page lists administrators
who sign in with a password only ("1 administrator signs in with a password only:" and their
email). Ask each of them to turn on two-factor sign-in. The checklist looks only at two-factor
sign-in: an administrator with a passkey but without two-factor sign-in is still listed, because
their password alone still signs them in.

<Aside type="note">
An administrator who signs in **as a customer** (to help them) cannot see or change the
customer's password, two-factor sign-in, recovery codes, passkeys or signed-in devices. The page
says: "These settings belong to the customer. Go back to your own account to change yours."
</Aside>

## Passkeys

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> A passkey lets you sign in with your fingerprint,
your face, your device's PIN or a security key, instead of typing a password and a code. Passkeys
follow a standard called WebAuthn: the secret part of a passkey never leaves your device or
security key, so there is nothing a fake sign-in page could steal.

A passkey is an extra way in, not a replacement: your password (and two-factor code) keep
working. You can add several, for example one on your laptop and one on your phone.

### Before you start

- Open the panel by its **host name over HTTPS**, such as `https://panel.example.com`.
  Passkeys do not work on an IP address, and over plain `http://` they work only on
  `localhost`.
- A passkey works **only on the address you add it on**. One added on `panel.example.com` does
  not work when you open the panel as `server1.example.com`. Add it on the address you always
  use.
- Your browser and device must support passkeys: for example Windows Hello, Touch ID or Face ID,
  Android's screen lock, a password manager that keeps passkeys, or a USB or NFC security key. If
  they cannot be used, the **Passkeys** card says: "This browser cannot use passkeys here.
  Passkeys need HTTPS and a browser that supports them."

### Add a passkey

<Steps>

1. Open **Your account → Security**. In the **Passkeys** card, choose **Add a passkey**.

2. Give it a **Name** that tells you which device it is on, such as `Laptop`, `Phone` or
   `Security key` (up to 60 characters; left empty, it is called "Passkey").

3. Enter **Your password** and choose **Create the passkey**.

4. Your browser or system asks you to confirm with your fingerprint, face, PIN or security key.
   Follow its steps within 5 minutes; after that the panel says "That took too long. Try
   again."

</Steps>

The passkey is now listed with its name, when you added it ("Added …") and when you last used
it ("last used …", or "not used yet"). The audit log records "Passkey added".

To remove one, choose the bin icon next to it and confirm ("Remove the passkey …? It will no
longer sign you in."). Remove a passkey when you lose or give away the device it is on. The
panel cannot delete it from the device or password manager itself; do that there if you want.
The audit log records "Passkey removed".

### Sign in with a passkey

On the sign-in page, choose **Sign in with a passkey** (under "or", below the **Sign in**
button). Your browser offers the passkeys it has for this panel's address; choose yours and
confirm with your fingerprint, face, PIN or security key. You do not type an email, a password
or a code.

A passkey counts as **both factors** at once: something you have (the device) and something you
are or know (your fingerprint, face or PIN, which the device checks before it answers). So it
signs you in even when two-factor sign-in is on.

As with a password, signing in from an address you have not used in the last 90 days sends a
**Sign-in from a new address** alert. The sign-in is in the audit log (`auth.login`, with
`"method": "passkey"`), and the device shows a **Passkey** badge in the list of
[signed-in devices](#sessions).

The **Sign in with a passkey** button shows only in a browser that can use passkeys on this
page, so it is missing on plain `http://` (except `localhost`).

### When a passkey does not work

| The panel says | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "Passkeys cannot be used on this address. Open the panel by its host name over HTTPS." | You opened the panel by its IP address. Use its host name with `https://`. |
| "The passkey could not be used. Try again, or sign in with your password." | Your device's answer was refused, for example because the passkey was removed in the panel. Sign in with your password, then check the **Passkeys** list. |
| "That took too long. Try again." | More than 5 minutes passed between the panel's request and your device's answer. |
| "This passkey is added already." | This device or security key already has a passkey for your account. |
| "That passkey is gone already." | It was removed already, perhaps in another browser tab. |

If your browser finds no passkey at all, check that you opened the panel on the same address
where you added it. If you close the browser's passkey window, nothing happens and no error is
shown. Failed passkey sign-ins count toward the [limit on attempts](#too-many-attempts) from one
address.

## Sign-in alerts

When you sign in from an address you have not signed in from in the last 90 days, with a
password or a passkey, ekPanel sends you a **Sign-in from a new address** alert with that
address. The very first sign-in of an account does not count.

The alert appears under the bell in the panel, and in the channels you choose under **Your
account → Notifications** (email, Telegram, Discord, Slack or a webhook). See
[Notifications](/guides/admin/notifications/). A sign-in with a recovery code sends an alert of
its own as well: see [Recovery codes](#recovery-codes).

If you did not sign in yourself, change your password right away (which also signs out every
other device) and turn on two-factor sign-in.

## Passwords

- A password has **at least 10 characters**. Longer is better; a password manager can make one.
- Passwords are stored only as argon2id hashes, never in readable form.
- To change it, open the **Password** card, enter your **Current password** and a **New
  password**, and choose **Change password**. This signs you out on every other device; this
  one stays signed in.
- An administrator can give a customer a new random password with **New password** under
  **Customers**. It is shown only once, and the customer is signed out everywhere. It does not
  change the customer's two-factor setting. See [Customers](/guides/admin/customers/).

### Too many attempts

After 10 failed sign-ins within 15 minutes, from one address or for one email address, sign-in
is paused: "Too many attempts. Wait 15 minutes and try again." A wrong or used recovery code
counts as a failed sign-in, and so does a failed passkey sign-in (for its address). Every failed
sign-in is written to the audit log.

## Sessions

- Signing in keeps you signed in on that device for **7 days**, and each time you use the panel
  the 7 days start again.
- An administrator signed in as a customer is limited to one hour.
- Opening a [web terminal](/guides/apps/terminal/) asks for your password (and two-factor code)
  again if you have not given it in the last 15 minutes, also when you signed in with a passkey.

<Badge text="next release" variant="note" /> The **Signed-in devices** card lists every browser
where your account is signed in now, the most recently used first. Each line shows:

- the browser and system, such as `Firefox · Linux` or `Chrome · Android`;
- **This device** for the browser you are using now;
- **Passkey** when that device signed in with a passkey;
- **An administrator** when an administrator is signed in as you (on a customer's account);
- the address, when it signed in ("signed in …") and when it was last seen ("seen …"). The last
  seen time is updated at most every 5 minutes.

To sign out one device, choose **Sign out** on its line. It is signed out at once, without a
question, and the audit log records "Device signed out". The device you are using has no
**Sign out** button in the list: sign out of the panel as usual instead.

To sign out every device except this one, choose **Sign out all other devices**. Do that, and
change your password, if you see a device or address you do not recognise.

Session cookies are `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax` and (with HTTPS) `Secure` with the `__Host-`
prefix, so scripts on a page and other sites cannot read or send them.

## What is not there yet

- **API tokens** for scripts and other programs come with the next release: see
  [API tokens](/guides/security/api-tokens/). Signing out devices does not revoke them.
- An administrator cannot reset **another administrator's** two-factor sign-in in the panel;
  use `ekpanel reset-2fa` on the panel's server.
- A recovery code cannot be used to open a web terminal.
- Recovery codes and the list of signed-in devices were tried in a browser on a local panel: a
  recovery code signed in once and was refused the second time, the card then showed 9 codes
  left, and signing out another device ended its session. Resetting a customer's two-factor
  sign-in and passkeys are covered by the panel's automated tests (passkeys with a virtual
  authenticator). **Adding a passkey with a real phone, laptop or security key has not been
  tried yet.**
