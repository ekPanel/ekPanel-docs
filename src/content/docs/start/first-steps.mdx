This page walks you through your first half hour with a freshly installed panel: from the
first sign-in to a website that is online and backed up.

## Before you start

- ekPanel is [installed](/start/install/), and you have the address and the setup token the
  installer printed.
- For a website with a trusted certificate: a domain whose DNS record (A, and AAAA for IPv6)
  points at your server.
- An authenticator app on your phone, such as Google Authenticator, Aegis or 1Password.

## 1. Create the administrator

<Steps>

1. Open the panel's address. On a panel without a domain, your browser warns about the
   self-signed certificate; accept it once.

2. The page **Welcome to ekPanel** opens. Paste the **Setup token**. If you lost it, run
   `sudo cat /var/lib/ekpanel/setup-token` on the server.

3. Fill in **Your name**, **Email** and **Password**. The password needs at least 10
   characters; a short sentence is easy to remember and hard to guess.

4. Click **Create administrator**. You are signed in.

</Steps>

You do this only once. Next time, the panel shows **Sign in**, where you enter your email
and password. The panel's language follows your browser at first; you can switch between
English and বাংলা at any time.

## 2. Turn on two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication means signing in needs a 6-digit code from your phone as well as
your password. An administrator can do everything on every server, so turn it on now.

<Steps>

1. In the menu, under **Your account**, open **Security**.

2. Click **Turn on two-factor authentication**.

3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app. If you cannot scan it, type the key shown
   under it into the app instead.

4. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows, and click **Confirm and turn on**.

</Steps>

On the same page you can change your password and **Sign out all other devices**.

<Aside type="caution">
Keep your phone, or a backup of your authenticator app, safe. Without the code you cannot
sign in.
</Aside>

## 3. Look around: dashboard and server

The **Dashboard** greets you and shows whether everything is running. Until you are done, a
**Getting started** card lists three steps: create the administrator, connect a server,
and turn on two-factor authentication. The installer already connected your server, so
after the step above all three are ticked and the card goes away.

Open **Servers** in the menu. Your server is listed under its host name and shows
**Online**. Click it to see:

- **System**: host name, operating system, kernel, architecture, agent version and when it
  connected.
- Live **CPU**, **Memory**, **Disk** and **Network** charts. Pick a **Time range** of
  **Live**, **1 hour**, **24 hours**, **7 days** or **30 days**.
- **Services** such as Caddy, PostgreSQL and cron, with their state and a **Restart**
  button for the ones ekPanel may restart.
- The websites, databases and apps on this server, the firewall, and more.

Slow work, such as restarting a service or installing PHP, runs as a background task. The
task list in the panel shows it as **Waiting**, **Running**, **Done** or **Failed**.

## 4. Add your first website

<Steps>

1. Open **Websites** and click **Add website**.

2. Enter the **Domain**, for example `example.com`. Leave **Also answer on www.example.com**
   ticked if you want both addresses to work.

3. Pick the **Server**. You have only one for now.

4. Choose the **Type of site**:
   - **PHP** for WordPress, Laravel and other PHP applications. Then pick the **PHP
     version**; the recommended one is marked. A version that is not on the server yet is
     installed automatically, which can take a few minutes the first time.
   - **Static** for plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript files.

5. Choose the **SSL certificate (HTTPS)**:
   - **Automatic, free**: Let's Encrypt, renewed automatically. The domain's DNS must point
     at this server.
   - **Self-signed**: for testing, or domains that are not on the public internet. Browsers
     show a warning.

6. For a PHP site you can tick **Install WordPress** to get WordPress right away. The
   [WordPress guide](/guides/websites/wordpress/) explains the details.

7. Click **Create website**.

</Steps>

The website's page shows **Applying…** for a few seconds while the agent sets it up, then
**Live**. Click **Visit site**: you see a page that says your domain is ready.

If the site shows **Problem**, the page explains what went wrong. Fix the cause (often the
domain's DNS) and click **Try again**. More in the [websites guide](/guides/websites/websites/).

## 5. Put a page online

Each website has its own folder, `public_html`, in the home folder of the site's own Linux
account. The page you just saw is `public_html/index.html`.

<Steps>

1. On the website's page, click **Files**.

2. Open `public_html`.

3. Drag your `index.html` (and any other files) from your computer onto the list, or click
   **Upload**. When asked, choose **Replace it** to overwrite the welcome page.

4. Reload your website in the browser.

</Steps>

You can also edit a file right in the browser: click a text file's name to open it in the
editor, then click **Save** (or press `Ctrl+S`). For many files, or to work from your own editor, use SFTP; see the
[files guide](/guides/websites/files/).

## 6. Set up backups

Backups are encrypted and deduplicated, and cover every website and database on a server.

<Steps>

1. Open **Backups** and click **Add backup location**.

2. Pick the **Server**.

3. Under **Where to keep the backups**, choose:
   - **This server**: fast to set up, but lost if the server's disk is lost.
   - **S3-compatible storage**: Amazon S3, Backblaze B2, Cloudflare R2, Wasabi, MinIO and
     others. Safer, because the backups are off the server. Fill in the **Endpoint**,
     **Bucket**, **Access key ID** and **Secret access key**.

4. Choose **How often** (every hour, every day, every week, or only when you start it), the
   time (**At**) and **How many to keep**.

5. Click **Create**.

6. The panel shows the **Recovery key** once. Save it somewhere away from this server, such
   as a password manager, then tick **I have saved the recovery key**.

7. Click **Back up now** to make the first backup.

</Steps>

<Aside type="danger" title="Save the recovery key">
The recovery key is the only key to your backups. ekPanel does not keep it. Without it,
nobody, including you, can read or restore them, for example after rebuilding the server.
With S3 backups and this key, you can also
[rebuild a lost server's websites on another server](/guides/websites/rebuild/).
</Aside>

## What next

  failed backup.
- Look at the [security overview](/guides/security/overview/) and turn on the
  [firewall](/guides/security/firewall/).
- Host other people: [customers and packages](/guides/admin/customers/).
